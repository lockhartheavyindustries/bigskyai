# Vision

`bigskyai` exists so that Big Sky AI, Brian Lockhart's AI company in Bozeman, Montana, sells only AI work it has already made run for real people, and shows that work in public.
It serves owner-led small businesses first, whose owner feels a workflow's pain personally and can say yes without a committee, and trusted households and small groups that want a private assistant in the channels they already use.
Households stay served through Ralph Private even at a loss, because people hear about Ralph from friends who use it and those households are how business owners find Ralph Pro, and Ralph Private keeps its place only while it remains that doorway.
It turns what Big Sky AI has built, run, and repaired into public evidence, a short ladder of offers, and one way to start a conversation.
It owns exactly one thing: Big Sky AI's root vision - who the company serves, what it offers, how it works, and what it claims in public - which every Big Sky AI product and customer repository inherits.

## Receipts before claims

Showing the work is a habit: every public claim traces to something that runs, ran, or is plainly labeled as not yet running.
Each showcase carries its true status - installation or demonstration - and never reads as more finished, more used, or more available than it is.
A showcase is evidence for an offer, not the product itself, and the front page shows only work that runs for real people and the offers built on it.
Illustrative figures are labeled illustrative, and a client's dollar amounts are never shown.
Customer outcomes, testimonials, metrics, partnerships, and prices appear only when an approved, current source backs them.
Technical write-ups on how the systems actually work, such as how each person's memory stays private, are published on bigskyai.ai as receipts for owners and builders alike.
A page that advertises something that has stopped moving comes down or is relabeled, rather than waiting to be noticed.
Every public page passes `just check` - links, privacy denylist, required metadata, and exactly one analytics beacon - before it merges, and a merge to `main` is what publishes it.

## Private stays private

Raw chats, private memory, phone numbers, real names, credentials, and unapproved media never enter this repository or the site.
A person's story is published only with recorded approval of its exact text, and anonymization is never reversed or hinted at.
Clients are anonymous by default, and a showcase names a client only with that client's approval.
The first real client's case study is published as a named showcase with that client's approval, because a named local business is the strongest proof the company can offer.
Anonymity covers who a client is, never what the system does: a showcase never names or hints at its client, while its capabilities, status, and illustrative figures stay exact.
Work that has not been revealed stays off every public surface - no teaser, no registry row, no link - until its reveal.
This repository holds public content and rules, never secrets, and nothing here changes a deployment, domain, account, billing, or credential without separate owner approval.

## A ladder of care, not a menu of tasks

Big Sky AI sells one ladder a client climbs as each rung earns it: an AI-led website optimization, a workflow audit, a fixed-scope build, ongoing care, and a private assistant that helps run the operation.
The first rung is a complete, AI-led optimization of the client's website - search, agent discoverability, and lead capture - that makes the site an AI-enabled front door for the rest of the business.
Big Sky AI does not take HTML edits, frontend-only jobs, or cosmetic redesigns, and social work is a governed publishing workflow, never an open-ended content treadmill.
A vague request such as "make my website better" is a diagnostic trigger, not a work order, and tasks are never quoted a la carte.
Reliability is what clients buy: verification, watchdogs, and silent-failure hunting are the product, which is why ongoing care is the recurring spine rather than an add-on.
The ladder is published now, each rung with what the client gets and a way to start a conversation, and without prices.
Prices are set and published only after the first real client's engagement becomes a working case study, and then beside specific deliverables, above commodity AI help, and never moving down in public.
Big Sky AI can be a client's whole technical department - domains, email, website, social scheduler, and assistant, held and run under Big Sky AI's accounts for one monthly fee, each with a documented path for handing it to the client - or it can work inside accounts the client owns through access the client can revoke.
The first market is the Gallatin Valley: local service businesses and contractors first, professional offices second.

## The products inherit this vision

Ralph, Installation 001, is Big Sky AI's flagship proof: a private assistant in production for a friends-and-family group since early 2026.
This is Big Sky AI's root vision: ralph-as-product, ralph-hermes, and every customer repository inherit it, may narrow it, and never contradict it, so a product change that conflicts with it is resisted in the product repository too.
ralph-as-product defines the Ralph product and its Private and Pro editions, ralph-hermes owns its Hermes implementation, openclaw-config owns production Ralph, and each customer repository owns that customer's product.
This repository sets company policy and never defines a product's behavior, and what the site says about a product follows that product's vision and its current, proven state.
Every install runs as a managed service on a dedicated machine for each client that Big Sky AI operates, and the site promises no install on a client's premises.
Capabilities proven inside Ralph graduate into their own installation or product or retire with a recorded tombstone, and the site shows only what graduated.
Consumer app prototypes are shelved rather than sold: they leave bigskyai.ai and keep only their row in the lockhartheavyindustries.com registry.

## Big Sky AI runs on its own products

Big Sky AI runs its own business on its own products first, and its business assistant is where a business capability proves itself before any client receives it.
That assistant is public AI staff: it appears on the About page and runs the site's intake, asking the diagnostic questions and booking the conversation with a human, as its own principal with no reach into company or client private material.
It drafts Big Sky AI's social posts from what actually shipped, and after a sustained run of drafts approved unedited it may publish under Big Sky AI's own name without per-post approval, with a receipt for every post and a kill switch, while a customer's name stays approval-gated however long its record.
Every workflow is run by hand first and automated only after the manual version has run long enough to trust, so letting the assistant draft and send a fixed-scope proposal straight to a prospect, with Brian reviewing after, is where intake is headed, not where it starts.

## A small business with one accountable owner

Brian is the named, accountable owner behind every system: he scopes it, approves it, and answers for it.
Big Sky AI is meant to grow into a small business that can include Brian's family as human assistants, and a second human who handles care monitoring, client check-ins, and routine fixes under Brian's review is welcome.
Agents do most of the building and operating, and they work through the coordination contract - issue, isolated branch, pull request, owner review - never through ambient authority.
Depth for each client outranks the number of clients, and the client count grows only with the human care capacity to keep them.

## Scope

Big Sky AI is not a web shop, a frontend or SEO task contractor, a social-media content agency, a consumer-app studio, a SaaS, or a reseller of generic chatbots.
Nothing it installs takes autonomous financial, legal, health, or safety-critical action, and no showcase gives investment, legal, or medical advice.
Personal writing belongs on brianlockhart.com and the permanent registry of shipped projects on lockhartheavyindustries.com, and bigskyai.ai is the only one of the three with offers and calls to action.
This file is public, so it names no client, machine, credential, or unpublished price.

A change aligns when it makes Big Sky AI's proof more legible to an owner-operator, narrows a public claim to what actually runs, or moves a client one honest rung up the ladder toward an AI-enabled business, while keeping private material private.
A change should be resisted when it claims a capability, customer, outcome, or price the evidence does not back, advertises a shelved or unrevealed project, turns the company toward frontend, content, or consumer-app work, exposes private material, or lets a product or customer repository contradict this vision.
