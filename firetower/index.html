The engagement · Client 001

The data existed. The operating model didn’t.

Client 001 is a private wealth manager running a small family office: a leveraged income portfolio with real margin, real distributions, and no research desk behind it—one person responsible for a family’s capital, with obligations that don’t pause for market hours.

The raw material was all there — live positions and margin metrics inside the brokerage, distribution announcements scattered across issuer sites, market catalysts across news and social feeds, and the house rules living in documents and memory. Six sources of truth, zero shared clock. Every volatile morning meant manual math under pressure.

Firetower turns fragmented brokerage data into a governed portfolio operating system.

Every figure on this page is illustrative — the shape is real, the dollars are not.

The operating model

Observe. Interpret. Plan. Govern.

Four capabilities, one governed workflow. Ordinary broker tools stop at the first.

01

Observe

One live portfolio truth.

  • Live account, holding, P&L, and margin data across taxable and retirement views
  • Quote freshness, cache age, and stale-source detection on every tile
  • Concentration, leverage, and risk-band history
  • Web dashboard on the client’s private network, plus chat delivery
02

Interpret

Explain what actually moved.

  • Pre-market, midday, and closing briefings — portfolio-specific, not generic news
  • Verified catalysts for unusual holding moves; every claim carries a source
  • Macro, rates, volatility, and sector context read through this portfolio
  • Explicit degradation when research is unavailable — never silent
03

Plan

Model the decision before making it.

  • Broker-backed margin previews for proposed buys, sales, and withdrawals
  • Post-trade LTV and excess-liquidity estimates, with solver-computed safe ceilings
  • Income gained or sacrificed by any proposed change
  • Further −10% and −20% stress scenarios on demand
04

Govern

Keep the strategy intact under pressure.

  • Green / Yellow / Orange / Red operating modes with explicit rules per mode
  • DRIP, buying, withdrawal, and deleveraging behavior gated by mode
  • Concentration overrides and distribution-cut monitoring
  • Data-source health checks, post-report audits, human approval for every action

The governed playbook

Four operating modes. Written once, enforced everywhere.

The client’s rules, moved out of their head and into one tested definition — thresholds, modes, and a hard dollar floor. The same definition renders the dashboard, constrains the what-if solver, shapes briefing language, and wakes the watchdog. Two languages, one source: nothing on the desk can disagree about risk.

LTV < 62% GREEN Operate within plan. Reinvestment runs, buys allowed, withdrawals per schedule.
62 – 70% YELLOW New buys gated by the solver ceiling. Every briefing names the mode. Watchdog cadence tightens.
70 – 75% ORANGE Pre-planned de-risking steps surface, sized and ranked. Withdrawal capacity re-modeled daily.
LTV > 75% RED Reinvestment off, buys off. Alerts escalate until the client acts or stands down explicitly.
Cushion floor HARD $ A dollar line that triggers regardless of ratios — because percentages feel abstract and dollars don’t.

Case study · a high-volatility session

Rotation, not crash.

Semiconductors fell nearly 5% while the broad market finished positive — and the portfolio’s concentrated AI exposure amplified the day into its largest drawdown of the quarter. The exact scenario the playbook was written for.

The system identified a sector rotation, not a systemic crash, quantified what leverage did to net liquidation value, and tracked excess liquidity from the morning low through the afternoon recovery. When the bounce came, governance held: a recovery is not new buying capacity. The client made one pre-planned move, on time, with the evidence attached — and next-morning acceptance checks reconciled every share count and balance against broker records.

Trust architecture

Trust is a feature, not fine print.

T—1

Freshness

Every endpoint carries a timestamp and cache age. Stale data is labeled stale. A source failure cannot silently masquerade as a live value.

T—2

Provenance

Distributions confirm from issuers or press releases. Market claims carry sources. Broker-reported risk stays separate from modeled interpretation.

T—3

Deterministic delivery

Core reports never depend on a model completing. Research degrades independently without blocking the facts. Every scheduled report is audited after delivery.

T—4

Human control

Broker access is read-only. Previews never submit orders. AI researches; it doesn’t get to assert or act. Material actions require explicit approval.
